# Five-person shopping usability test

This is a prepared protocol, not a claim that a human study has happened. Recruit five people who have not used Veelad. Include English and Arabic readers and both laptop and phone users. Test staging; no payment is part of this study.

Use labels P1–P5 rather than names or emails. Each participant uses an account they control, and enters their own password privately. Ask them to think aloud. Do not explain the interface during a task; record where they hesitate. Screenshots/recordings require their separate agreement and must omit passwords, personal addresses and private designs.

Ask whether they want to enable aggregate measurement through the footer's Privacy choices. Declining does not prevent the usability test. Explain that the dashboard counts events, not unique people, paid sales or a statistically reliable conversion rate.

## Five tasks, about 20 minutes per person

1. Find an original design you would consider buying. Switch between browsing designs and products, then filter to the product type you want. Explain why you chose it.
2. On a product with a reviewed artist layout, change the name/message and permitted colour. Generate a preview, check the actual wording, select the product option, and add the personalised version to the bag. Ask what they think the preview does and does not include.
3. Find the materials, dimensions and delivery/return information. If details are unconfirmed, ask what they would do next. Do not supply missing specifications.
4. Review the bag. Explain the product, variant, personalisation and price. Change quantity or remove the item if they want. Do not submit a payment or real order.
5. Find the full editor, save a draft and return to it. Ask which controls felt familiar and which needed explanation.

## Record observations

Use `observations.csv`. For every task, record completion without help, time in seconds, the exact point of confusion and what the participant expected. Do not manufacture results or infer a percentage lift from five people.

Prioritise blockers first: wrong item/text/variant in the bag, missing preview, impossible navigation, misleading delivery or a lost draft. Then fix repeated hesitation observed in two or more sessions. After changes, rerun the affected tasks with fresh participants where possible.

The administrator's Catalogue quality screen supports content review; Shopping insights supports opted-in event totals and coarse speed/error counts. Neither replaces observing actual people or confirms supplier readiness.
